On-board control shipped

The battery-power-tracking core: filter (τ 2 s) → freezing deadband (±25 W) → clamped PI with tracking anti-windup → asymmetric slew (+2/−5 %/s). Modes MANUAL / SOLAR (0 W target) / SOLAR+ (bounded battery draw or forced charging) / REMOTE (phone target, degrades to SOLAR after 10 s of silence). Latched reserve-SOC floor with hysteresis. Zero overshoot and ±deadband tracking through 300–1500 W solar swings, proven with committed graphs. One C++17 core, byte-identical on desktop and ESP32.

Safety & protection shipped relay logic: bench gate A5

MANUAL is the hardware default: a normally-de-energized relay held only by a toggling heartbeat — a crash stops the edges and the helm is yours. 15 fail-safe requirements (stale data, watchdog, brownout, no-throttle-restore-after-boot) all end at zero automatic throttle. The protection envelope adds staged voltage-sag backoff (50% / 15% / stop, debounced, hysteresis on release), a discharge-current cap under the BMS rating, and battery-temperature derating — enforced below the mode logic, whatever any controller wants.

Hardware & drivers shipped bench gates A1–A7

Victron VE.Direct (SmartShunt + MPPT), NMEA 0183 GNSS, and the GP8403 0–5 V throttle DAC — byte-fed, fixed-buffer parsers, 100% desktop-tested; drivers are plugins behind small interfaces, no brand lock-in. The ESP32-S3 firmware binds them to real UARTs and I2C, runs the 10 Hz loop under a task watchdog, opens a SoftAP, serves the companion app from its own flash and exposes the HTTP API (GET /telemetry, POST /remote). All of it compiles in CI; the gated bench protocol is what stands between it and the water. Boat-side NVS config sync and flash telemetry logging are the named next steps of that milestone.

Trip planner (app) shipped

Hour-by-hour trip simulation against the Open-Meteo forecast with a clear-sky fallback — PV production, propulsion, hotel load and SOC with the firmware's reserve philosophy. Charts, per-day cards, offline-capable and installable. The app is the boat's control panel: Plan · Voyage · Boat · Model · Setup tabs, served from GitHub Pages or from the ESP32 itself.

Voyage planner shipped

Waypoints on an OpenStreetMap route editor (tap to add, marker taps toggle anchorages), then a dynamic program over every departure time and power setting — waves, wind and current vectors included, sampled per segment on long routes. Out come the SAFE/POSSIBLE/INFEASIBLE verdict with six named gates, arrival SOC with calibrated uncertainty, the arrival-time↔SOC tradeoff curve, emergent solar stops, and a per-day energy ledger. Environment magnitudes for waves/aero are physics priors until sea-trial data refines them — by design, never a hidden multiplier.

Learning shipped feeds on real telemetry

Telemetry logs run through steady-block gating into an NNLS monotone hull refit, residuals against the prior model, CUSUM drift detection (fouling shows up in the numbers), and calibrated error quantiles that turn the SAFE gates honest. The Model tab shows the learned curve, the recommended cruise band (the EnergyKnee), and the live solar-equilibrium speed. Geographic bias bins and provider-skill tracking are implemented and wired; they populate once the boat logs positioned telemetry — the machinery waits on data, not on code.

Engineering discipline shipped

100% line coverage in both languages, enforced in CI on every push; a headless-browser smoke test drives the real pages; 13 simulator scenarios encode the acceptance criteria as tests; the Helios 11 case study validates the physics against 3,000 real nautical miles. Every claim on this page links to code or a doc in the repository.

Deliberately not built yet

RANGE/ARRIVAL on-boat modes (Milestone 4 by design), IMU wave features (Wave-1 hardware), H3 geographic bins, FES2022 offline tide packs, MapLibre offline map packs, and the SolarHelm Vessel Designer — all documented in the roadmap with the reason each one waits.
