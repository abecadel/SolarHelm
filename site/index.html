How it works

One control variable changes everything

A conventional cruise control holds speed and drains the battery to do it. SolarHelm targets 0 W of battery power and continuously trims the motor so propulsion follows solar production — no forecast in the loop, no coupling to any charger; the physics does the accounting. Target −200 W and the boat may borrow from the pack for speed; target +500 W and it must keep charging while it moves. At the reserve state of charge the pack simply can't be drawn down further.

 shunt (~1 Hz) ─► filter τ2s ─► deadband ±25 W (freezes cmd)
   ─► PI + tracking anti-windup ─► slew +2/−5 %/s ─► clamps ─► throttle

Each stage kills a failure mode: the filter keeps wave ripple off the prop, the freezing deadband stops hunting, the anti-windup gives zero overshoot on solar surges, and down-slew is 2.5× faster than up because losing sun is a safety event. Underneath, a protection envelope enforces the hardware's limits (staged voltage-sag backoff, current cap, temperature derating) regardless of what any controller wants. MANUAL is the hardware default: a relay held by a toggling heartbeat — a crash stops the edges and the helm is yours. Proof with graphs: simulation results, full theory: docs/CONTROL_THEORY.md.

Buying

Three build levels, three gates

Buy in waves, gated by tests — never all at once. Wave 1 is bench electronics; Wave 2 (motor, controller, battery) only after the bench passes and the motor head is opened to confirm its topology; Wave 3 (solar) after sea trials produce a real efficiency curve. Prices in PLN, snapshot 2026-08-29 — links and verification flags in the full guide.

Cheap is fine for ESP32 boards, GNSS modules, enclosures and standard residential panels (6× cheaper per watt than "marine", same cells — check for IEC 61701 salt-mist rating). Don't cheap out on the shunt, the fuse/contactor/kill chain, BMS quality or cable sizing. Avoid flexible panels except where nothing else fits.

Install

Wiring and commissioning

 (+) Battery ── main fuse ── battery switch ──┬── MPPT battery (+)
      │        (at the post,                  ├── contactor ── motor ctrl ── motor
      │         before anything)              └── DC/DC ── hotel loads
 (−) Battery ── SmartShunt ──┬────────────────┴── common negative bus
                             └── EVERY load's return goes through
                                 the shunt, or SOC lies

 GP8403 DAC ─► hardware clamp ─► relay NO ─┐
 Manual throttle pot ─────────── relay NC ─┼─► motor controller
 relay common ─────────────────────────────┘   throttle input

Four rules, no exceptions: main fuse first at the battery post, sized by calculation; the shunt sees every return; the kill switch drives the contactor directly and never passes through SolarHelm; panel strings stay under the MPPT's Voc limit at cold temperature. The relay is normally closed on the manual pot — with SolarHelm dead, the helm works as if it were never installed.

Commissioning is three gated phases with written acceptance criteria — bench (scope the DAC's cold-boot 0 V, prove the relay drops on a firmware hang, watchdogs, HIL), motor bench (autopsy the motor before buying the controller, calibrate prop-off, verify every fault path in hardware), then the water (dock trials, SOLAR maiden, efficiency runs that produce your real hull curve). Never proceed on a failed gate. Full protocol: docs/HARDWARE_TEST_PLAN.md, wiring detail: docs/WIRING.md.

Safety first. SolarHelm generates a low-power throttle signal only; it never switches propulsion current, an independent kill switch always remains, and it is not a certified navigation system. Read docs/SAFETY.md before wiring anything.
